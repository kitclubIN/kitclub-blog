The 2026/27 season has brought plenty of new football shirts, but not every new release deserves the same attention.

This is not a ranking of which club is having the best season on the pitch. It is purely about the shirts: how they look, where the ideas come from and how well those ideas have been executed.

And because new kits continue to appear and opinions change once shirts are seen in real matches, this is best treated as a snapshot of the season so far.

## Liverpool — Home

Liverpool's 2026/27 home shirt leans into the club's history, with adidas revisiting the visual language of the famous 1989–91 era.

The result is recognisably Liverpool without simply copying an old shirt. The deeper red and geometric detailing give the design a strong retro character while keeping the overall shirt modern.

It is a good example of how a heritage reference can work when it is treated as inspiration rather than a costume.

## Liverpool — Away

The away shirt takes the retro idea in a different direction.

The white base creates a much cleaner canvas, while the adidas Trefoil and historic Liver Bird treatment give the shirt a distinctly archival feel.

It is the kind of design that works because it does not need a complicated graphic to make an impression.

## Lyon — Away

Lyon's away shirt is one of the more distinctive releases of the season.

The burgundy-and-gold palette immediately separates it from the usual white, black and navy away shirts. The design also connects with Lyon's history and its association with silk and craftsmanship.

The important part is restraint. The shirt has a story behind it without filling every available surface with graphics.

## Arsenal — Away

Arsenal's away shirt draws from the club's famous 1990s “Bruised Banana” aesthetic.

The original is one of the most recognisable Arsenal shirts ever made, so revisiting that visual language comes with obvious expectations.

The 2026/27 version uses the reference without simply reproducing the old shirt, making it one of the season's more obvious examples of football nostalgia being turned into a modern release.

## Manchester United — Home

Manchester United's home shirt takes inspiration from the 1970s, with a clean red base, polo-style collar and striped cuffs.

The design also references the club's 1977 FA Cup-winning era.

It is a reminder that a traditional football shirt does not need an elaborate graphic to stand out. Strong proportions and familiar club colours can do most of the work.

## Borussia Dortmund — Home

Dortmund's home shirt keeps the unmistakable yellow base but adds a tonal graphic influenced by the industrial identity of the Ruhr region.

That connection between a club and its city is one of the strongest ways to make a football shirt feel specific rather than generic.

## Real Madrid — Away

Real Madrid's away shirt takes the club in a darker direction, using green as a major part of the colour palette.

It is a significant departure from the familiar white home-shirt identity and shows how an away shirt can experiment more freely while remaining immediately recognisable.

## What makes a great kit?

There is no objective formula for a great football shirt.

For this Journal, four things matter most:

### 1. Identity

You should be able to understand which club the shirt belongs to without being told.

### 2. A clear idea

The strongest kits usually have a reason behind their details — a historic shirt, a local reference, a stadium, a city or an important moment in the club's history.

### 3. Restraint

More graphics do not automatically mean a better shirt. Good colour balance and spacing can be more effective than adding another pattern.

### 4. Wearability

A football shirt exists in a stadium, but it also exists outside one. The best designs tend to work as clothing as well as teamwear.
