A good football jersey is about more than the club badge and colours.

When you are buying online, it can be difficult to judge quality from a few product photographs. The useful approach is to look for details that tell you how the jersey has actually been constructed.

## Start with the fabric

The fabric should match the purpose of the jersey.

Performance-oriented versions usually use lighter technical materials designed to manage moisture and allow movement. Supporter versions may use a slightly different construction that prioritises everyday comfort.

Neither approach is automatically better. What matters is whether the construction matches what you expect from the product.

## Look closely at the badge

The club badge is one of the easiest places to notice differences in construction.

Depending on the jersey, it may be embroidered, heat-applied, woven or made using another application method.

Look for clean edges, accurate colours and a badge that sits correctly on the shirt.

Small details matter because they are difficult to hide when you are wearing the jersey in person.

## Check the sponsor application

Sponsors should sit cleanly and evenly on the shirt.

Look at the edges of the print, spacing and alignment. On heat-applied graphics, inconsistent edges or obvious imperfections can be warning signs.

Again, the exact finish depends on the original kit design and the version you are buying.

## Inspect the collar and cuffs

Collars and sleeve cuffs receive a lot of wear, so their construction is worth checking.

Look for clean stitching, even seams and a shape that matches the product photographs.

A good jersey should look intentional from the front, back and sides rather than only from one carefully chosen angle.

## Pay attention to the inside

Product photographs sometimes focus entirely on the front of the jersey.

If possible, look for photographs of the inside, care label, neck label and stitching.

These details can reveal whether the construction is consistent throughout the garment.

## Beware of exaggerated claims

Terms such as “premium”, “top quality” and “1:1” do not tell you very much by themselves.

A better product description explains what actually changes: fabric, fit, badge construction, printing, stitching and other details.

Specific information is more useful than a quality claim with no explanation behind it.

## Quality is also about consistency

The best sign is not one impressive detail. It is consistency across the entire jersey.

The badge should be clean. The sponsor should be aligned. The seams should be tidy. The fabric should suit the version. The sizing should match the chart.

When those things come together, the jersey feels considered rather than simply printed.
